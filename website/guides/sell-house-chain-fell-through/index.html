House sale fallen through? Work out your next move

If a buyer withdraws or your chain breaks, first establish where the sale stopped and what you need to protect. Then compare relisting, another sale route or a direct offer against your actual deadline.

Prepared by the South Yorkshire Property Buyers team. General information for England and Wales; your solicitor should advise on your sale and any agreements.

Had contracts exchanged?

This is the first question to ask your solicitor. GOV.UK explains that an ordinary offer is not legally binding until exchange in England and Wales. If contracts have already exchanged, a buyer failing to complete is a different contractual situation. Seek advice before accepting a replacement offer.

Before exchange, the absence of a binding property sale does not mean the process has been free. Legal work and other charges may already have been incurred. A reservation, exclusivity, auction or agency agreement may also create separate obligations, so ask what remains payable and whether any terms restrict the next step.

Why did the sale stop?

Ask the agent and solicitor for the clearest explanation available. It helps identify whether the next buyer could face the same problem:

  • A chain or buyer funding issue: the house may not be the problem, but the next buyer’s position still needs checking.
  • A survey concern: obtain the findings you are entitled to receive and ask whether specialist advice is needed.
  • A title, lease or legal issue: your solicitor can explain what remains unresolved and whether it affects other buyers.
  • A changed decision or timetable: consider whether your onward plans and price expectations still fit.

Do not assume every buyer withdrawal can be solved by reducing the price, or that a cash buyer can ignore a legal or condition problem. Understanding it makes the comparison more useful.

What are your options after a failed sale?

Ask the current agent about another buyer

There may be previous interest worth following up. Ask about the buyer’s funding, chain and ability to meet your timetable. Check whether the existing agency agreement remains in force and whether any price or marketing changes are justified.

Resolve an issue before relisting

If a survey or title issue caused the withdrawal, find out what it would take to clarify or resolve it. Compare the expense and likely delay with selling subject to that known issue. Do not undertake substantial work based only on an unconfirmed diagnosis.

Consider auction

An auction is another route to assess, with its own marketing period, reserve, fees and legal conditions. A modern auction reservation differs from an unconditional sale contract. See the auction and direct sale comparison before instructing.

Compare a direct cash offer

A direct purchase removes the need for that buyer to sell another property first. It may suit someone whose onward plans depend on moving within a short period, or who cannot manage another round of viewings. The offer remains subject to its written terms and legal checks.

Can the previous legal work save time?

Some existing documents and answered enquiries may help explain the property, but a new buyer’s solicitor decides what they can use and what must be updated or repeated. Searches, survey reports and other documents can have reliance or validity limitations. Completion is not automatically imminent because the previous sale reached an advanced stage.

Tell us whether a solicitor is instructed, what remains outstanding and whether any survey or legal concern caused the withdrawal. We can discuss a realistic target date with the advisers involved. We do not promise 24 hour completion or a fixed number of days before assessing the position.

What should you include when comparing costs?

Separate costs already incurred from what each new route would add. Ask about abortive legal charges, agency or auction terms, new marketing, updated paperwork and mortgage costs. Include the ongoing bills while the property is held and any cost of changing your onward plans.

Council Tax does not automatically double in every failed sale situation. Empty home premiums, discounts and exceptions depend on the property and council’s decision. Check your bill and the official empty property guidance rather than building a sale decision around a blanket assumption.

Any applicable tax, mortgage redemption and other secured charges also affect your net proceeds. Ask your advisers to compare written figures for your own property.

What would we need to know?

  • The postcode, condition and whether the house is occupied.
  • Why the buyer withdrew, if known, and whether contracts exchanged.
  • Any existing agent, auction, reservation or exclusivity agreement.
  • Your deadline and whether you have an onward purchase or another commitment.
  • Who owns the house, who can authorise a sale and how far the legal work progressed.
