House Sale Fallen Through? — detailed guide

Explore the options, practical considerations and questions to ask before deciding how to sell. If you want to discuss your own property, you can ask us for an offer without an obligation to accept.

What actually happens when a sale falls through

The sale stops, and nothing about it was ever binding. In England and Wales neither you nor your buyer is committed until contracts are exchanged. Up to that moment either side can walk away, at no cost and with no notice.

That is the part most people find hardest. Months of viewings, negotiation and solicitor correspondence, plans built around a moving date, and none of it counted for anything. It is a genuinely bad day and it is worth saying so plainly before we get to the practical bits.

What follows is what it has cost you, what you can and can't recover, and the four routes open to you now. If you have already decided you want to skip the relisting cycle, our guide on selling your house quickly in South Yorkshire covers the chain free route step by step.

Why sales collapse before exchange

Knowing which of these happened to you matters, because it tells you whether the next buyer will hit the same wall.

Only the survey and the legal pack say anything about your property. The rest are about the chain, and a chain is the one thing you can remove.

What a collapsed sale has already cost you

Some of it is one-off. The part people miss is what keeps running every month the property sits there waiting for a second buyer.

Two things you won't be billed for: estate agent fees, if you were on a no sale, no fee agreement, and the buyer's survey, which was their cost. They have lost that fee too, which is why most buyers don't walk away lightly.

The monthly ones are what people underestimate. A sale that collapses and takes another few months to replace can cost more in holding costs than the abortive legal fees ever did, and if it tips the property past a year empty, the council tax doubles on top.

Tax, including any Capital Gains Tax, is your own responsibility and isn't covered by any cash buying service. Take independent tax advice if it applies to you.

Can you claim anything back from the buyer?

In almost every case, no. Until exchange of contracts, neither party owes the other anything. A buyer who has cost you months and several hundred pounds in legal fees can walk away owing nothing at all. It is one of the least forgiving features of how property sales work here.

Two things push against it. Reservation agreements ask the buyer for a deposit of around £500 to £1,000 when the offer is agreed, which you keep if they pull out without good reason. Lock-in agreements are a more formal version, rarely used in ordinary residential sales. Neither removes the risk, but a reservation agreement does filter out buyers who were never really committed. If you relist, ask your agent whether they use one.

Your four routes forward

Why being the end of the chain removes the risk

We buy with our own funds. That means there is no mortgage lender who can withdraw an offer, no survey that can be used to renegotiate, and no property we need to sell first in order to buy yours.

Take those three away and most of the reasons on the list above simply can't happen. What is left is conveyancing, which is why we complete in 7 to 28 days rather than the 22 weeks an open market sale typically takes.

On a sale that has already collapsed, most of that conveyancing is behind you. The title work is done, the property information forms are filled in, and the enquiries your buyer's solicitor raised have been answered. None of it needs doing twice. Our solicitor can pick the file up from where it stopped rather than opening a new one, and because we are buying with our own money there is no fresh mortgage application and no lender valuation to sit through. That is the part that normally takes the weeks, and it is the reason a fallen-through sale tends to complete at the fast end of 7 to 28 days. Where everything really is in place and the old sale was almost at completion, it can be 24 to 48 hours. How close yours is depends entirely on how far the previous buyer got, so we will tell you what is realistic for your file rather than quote you the best case.

We buy in any condition, including the problems that killed the last sale. You don't need to fix anything, clear anything, or arrange a single viewing. If the survey found damp, movement or knotweed, show us the report. It usually speeds things up.

How we work when a chain has broken

Tell us about the property and what happened to the last sale. We give you a written cash offer, usually the same day, valid for 14 days. It is our best offer on what you have told us, not an opening number we expect to negotiate up from.

We price each property on what it actually is. There is no percentage formula behind the figure. Once you accept, our solicitor speaks to yours and we work to the completion date you want, whether that is next week or three months out while you find somewhere to move to.
