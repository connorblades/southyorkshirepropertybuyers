What "urgent" actually means in property terms

The word "urgent" gets used loosely in property. On this page it means a date on a document: a court order, a financial settlement, a relocation letter, a council tax bill, a mortgage-arrears notice, that you can't move by talking to anyone. That distinction matters because every fast-sale route involves trade-offs, and the right trade-off depends entirely on whether you are working against a true external deadline or just feeling pressure.

A repossession hearing

Repossession runs through the courts and takes months, but once a hearing is listed the date is fixed. Aim to exchange before it: see selling before repossession.

A divorce financial order

A court order can require the home to be sold and the money split within months. The open market often isn't fast enough. See selling during divorce.

A relocation date

An employer transfer, a job with a fixed start date, a health condition needing a single-storey home, or care for a parent. Real deadlines, even if not legal ones.

An empty inherited house

Sheffield, Doncaster and Barnsley charge a 100% council tax premium once a home has stood empty for 12 months, and Rotherham too. See probate sale and inherited property.

A financial cliff

A mortgage product expiring, arrears building, or enforcement once a judgment is obtained. Every extra month of interest, council tax and insurance adds up. See can't afford the mortgage.

A sale that already collapsed

You've lost weeks to a buyer who pulled out, and roughly one in four agreed sales falls through. If your finances won't survive another cycle, the deadline is real.

The three urgent-sale routes, compared honestly

Best if the date is fixed

Direct cash buyer, 7 to 28 days

We buy with our own funds, so there is no lender, no down-valuation, no chain and no survey condition. The figure is below open market value, priced on the property rather than to a set percentage, and it is our best offer, first time, valid for 14 days. What sets the pace isn't us: it is your lender's redemption statement, typically 5 to 10 working days, your title, your ID paperwork and your solicitor's capacity. Some cases genuinely take longer than 7 days whatever we do, including probate before the Grant, mortgage arrears needing a redemption figure, sitting tenants, and a short lease that needs extending. No fees to you, and we can cover your legal fees if you use our panel solicitor, though you keep the right to instruct your own. Choose this when your deadline is inside 4 weeks, the property has any complication, or an open market sale has already failed.

Traditional auction, 6 to 10 weeks

Four to six weeks of marketing, then 28 days to complete after the hammer falls, and contracts exchange the instant it does. Q1 2026 sale rates ran at 67.6 to 72.2% across the major South Yorkshire rooms, including Mark Jenkinson, Auction House South Yorkshire, Pugh, Bond Wolfe and SDL. You pay an entry fee of £300 to £750, commission of 2 to 2.5% plus VAT and a legal pack, whether it sells or not. Don't mistake the modern method for a fast route: it runs 12 to 16 weeks, and its reservation fee comes out of what the buyer can afford to bid, which pushes the hammer price down by roughly the same amount. Our sell house at auction guide goes deeper.

Agent sale, 12 to 16 weeks at best

The 2026 average is 22 weeks. Twelve to sixteen is a stretch target you only hit by pricing realistically from day one, preparing the legal pack before listing rather than after an offer, insisting on sales progression, and screening offers for chain position and a mortgage in principle. Expect 95 to 100% of asking, less 1 to 2% plus VAT commission, £800 to £1,800 of legal fees and every week of carrying costs. Right if your deadline is 16 weeks or more, the property is turn-key in a strong postcode, and you can absorb the withdrawal and fall-through risk. Done badly, 26 to 30 weeks is normal.

Which urgent reasons actually justify a cash discount?

This is the question most other cash-buyer websites won't answer honestly. The cash offer trade, speed and certainty in exchange for a lower headline price, is the right answer for some urgent sellers and the wrong answer for others. Here is how we frame it:

Cash discount is justified when: repossession hearing inside 90 days (the alternative is losing the home to the lender at a typical 78 to 82% forced-sale recovery, with possession-order costs and arrears bolted on); divorce order with a 90-day or shorter window (court enforcement risk); empty-home premium about to activate where the executor is personally exposed; relocation date inside 8 weeks; property has any material complication that materially shrinks the open-market buyer pool; sale has already fallen through once and the financial position won't survive another 22-week cycle.

Cash discount isn't justified when: urgency is anxiety rather than a date on a document; the property is turn-key in a strong postcode with no complications and you have 16+ weeks; the maths run obviously in favour of the open market (run the worked example below). If we look at your situation and the open market is the right route, we will tell you that openly. We would rather lose your business than mis-sell it.

  • Estate agent sale at 100% of asking, completing in 22 weeks: £200,000 gross, less 1.5% + VAT agent fee (£3,600), less legal fees (£1,500), less 22 weeks of mortgage interest, council tax, insurance and maintenance (typically £4,000 to £7,000 on a £200,000 home) = roughly £187,000 to £190,000 net, if it completes.   • Traditional auction at 85% in 8 weeks: £170,000 gross, less 2% + VAT commission (£4,080), less entry and legal-pack fees (£600 to £1,200), less 8 weeks of carrying costs (£1,400 to £2,500) = roughly £162,000 to £164,000 net. Auction fees and eight weeks of carrying costs eat a large part of the difference between the hammer price and what actually reaches you.

The cash route trades headline value for certainty, a fixed date, no chain, no survey risk, and no fall-through exposure. Put our written figure next to the two nets above and you can see exactly what that trade costs you. For sellers with a hard deadline, that trade is straightforwardly worthwhile. For sellers without one, the open market often makes more sense, and we will tell you that. Note too that the agent figure above assumes 22 weeks of mortgage interest and running costs. If your home is mortgage-free, or would sell inside a few weeks, most of that cost never lands and the gap widens further in the agent's favour.

Government statistics put the average UK repossession timeline at 46 to 47 weeks from first court claim to actual repossession, roughly 10 to 11 months. Within that, the lender typically sends formal arrears letters from month three, issues a default notice at six to nine months, and applies to court at nine to twelve months. The court then gives a hearing date with a minimum of three days' notice. If an outright possession order is made, the court usually allows a further 28 days before eviction is enforceable (extendable to 56 days in hardship cases). If a bailiff warrant is then needed, that adds another 6 to 10 weeks. The latest realistic moment to sell privately is up to the day before the bailiffs arrive, because once you sell the legal charge is redeemed and the possession order collapses. But the practical safe window is to exchange contracts before the possession hearing, that gives the court evidence of an imminent completion and is the most effective way to ask the court to adjourn or suspend.
