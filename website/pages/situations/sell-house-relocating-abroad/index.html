What the two routes actually involve

The open market route has seven places it can stall. Ours has two parties in it.

7 things that can stop it

A chain sale

→✕→→
  • Your buyer applies for a mortgage, which the lender can still withdraw
  • The lender sends a surveyor, who can down-value or flag the condition
  • Searches come back and can raise something nobody expected
  • Enquiries go back and forth between four sets of solicitors
  • Your buyer's own sale has to hold together
  • So does their buyer's sale, and so on up the chain
  • Everyone has to be ready to exchange on the same day
2 parties, nothing behind us

Selling to us

→
  • No mortgage application, because we buy with our own funds
  • No lender's surveyor, so condition can't be used to renegotiate
  • No onward chain, because we aren't selling anything to buy yours
  • Your solicitor still does the searches and the legal work properly
  • You pick the completion date, and we work to it

That's why this route runs in weeks rather than months. The legal work still happens, the things that break sales don't.

The date is the problem, not the house

Most people who come to us before a move don't have a difficult property. They have a difficult deadline.

A flight is booked. A job starts on the first of the month. A visa has a window. The house is perfectly saleable, and that's exactly what makes the open market so frustrating, because a saleable house still takes as long as it takes.

An open market sale runs to a timetable nobody controls. Your buyer's mortgage offer, their survey, their own sale, and everyone above them in the chain. An agent can give you an average. Nobody can give you a date.

What usually goes wrong when you sell around a move

Four things, and we see all four regularly.

The sale outlives the move

You fly, and you're now running a UK sale from another time zone, with a solicitor you can only reach in a two hour window.

The chain breaks after you've gone

Roughly one in four agreed sales collapses before exchange. Starting again from abroad is a great deal harder than starting again from here.

You become a landlord by accident

The sale doesn't happen in time, so it gets let out as a stopgap. Years later it's still let, and now there's a tenancy to end first.

You carry two sets of costs

The UK mortgage, council tax, insurance and standing charges keep running while you're paying to set up somewhere else.

Your realistic options when you're leaving

There are three, and we're only one of them.

Sell with an estate agent. Usually the most money, and the right answer if your date is far enough out. Budget around 22 to 26 weeks from listing to completion, and accept that the date isn't yours to set. If you've got six months or more, start here.

Let it out. Keeps the asset and any growth. It also makes you a landlord in a country you don't live in, with the Renters' Rights Act, income tax on the rent, and a managing agent taking a cut. Some people want that. Many find out too late that they didn't.

Sell to us. Less than the open market would get you, and we won't pretend otherwise. What you get for that is the date. No chain, no mortgage offer to wait on, no viewings, and a completion you can plan a move around.

If your deadline is soft and the house is in good order, use an agent. We'd rather tell you that now than take a call in six weeks.

Selling a UK house when you're already overseas

If you've already gone, you can still sell. You don't need to be here.

The sale is handled by solicitors, and completion happens between them. Your solicitor will need to verify your identity and get signed paperwork from you, and they'll explain what that involves from the country you're in, because the requirements differ depending on where you are.

We can help you through it. Practically, that means we'll work to the hours you're actually awake for, we'll deal with whoever holds a key if the house is empty, and we won't ask you to fly back for a viewing.

There's no need to empty the house first either. Leave what you're not shipping. We buy properties as they stand, and clearing it afterwards is our job.

Agreeing now and completing later is fine

We are not only useful when something has to happen this month. A lot of people moving abroad want the sale settled before they start packing, then completion later, when the visa lands or the school year ends or the shipping is booked.

That works. You can accept an offer now and complete on a date months out if that is what the move needs. Our written offer is valid for 14 days as standard, so if your date is further away than that, tell us at the start and we will agree a timescale that holds rather than one that expires on you.

What we will not do is push you to complete faster than you want to. The date is the thing you came here for, and it is yours to set.

What it costs you, and what it saves

We buy below what the open market would pay. That's the trade, and it's the whole basis of the thing, so we say it plainly rather than burying it.

Against that: there are no estate agent fees, no listing costs, and we can cover your legal fees if you use our panel solicitor. There are no months of council tax, insurance and mortgage interest on a house you've already left. And there's no risk of a chain collapsing after you've handed back the keys to your rental and got on a plane.

Whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on how firm your date is. If it isn't firm, it probably isn't worth it.

Tax is not covered by our offer and stays your responsibility. Capital Gains Tax and non-resident reporting duties can both apply when you're leaving the UK or selling a UK property from abroad, and the deadlines are real. Take independent tax advice.
