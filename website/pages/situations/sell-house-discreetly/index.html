Quick answer: Yes, you can sell your house without it ever reaching the open market. Because we buy with our own money, there is no listing, no board, no advertising and no viewings. The only people who need to know are you, us and the solicitors. The one thing no honest buyer can hide is the sold price, which HM Land Registry publishes a few months after completion.

What a discreet sale actually means

A discreet sale means the property is never advertised. In practice:

  • No For Sale board outside the house at any point.
  • No portal listing. Nothing appears on Rightmove, Zoopla, OnTheMarket or an agent's window.
  • No open-market viewings. No strangers, no block viewings, no keys with an agent. One of our team usually wants to see the property once, at a time that suits you.
  • No agent calling your neighbours to drum up interest, which is a normal tactic and a common way word gets round.
  • No chain, so no other sellers, buyers or agents in the loop.

Why people ask for this

We are not going to ask you why, and you do not have to tell us. For what it is worth, the reasons we hear most are ordinary ones:

  • A separation or divorce that is not yet public, where a board outside the house would say more than either of you wants said.
  • Money trouble, where the whole street knowing feels worse than the debt itself.
  • A death in the family, where the last thing anyone wants is strangers walking round.
  • A landlord who does not want to unsettle a good tenant while the sale is still uncertain.
  • Illness, or a job change that has not been announced yet.
  • Simply not wanting your business to be everyone else's.

What we cannot keep private, and we will say so upfront

Anyone promising total secrecy is overselling. Three things are outside any buyer's control:

  1. The sold price becomes public. HM Land Registry records it and publishes it in their Price Paid data, usually two to six months after completion. That is the law and it applies to every sale in England and Wales. What a discreet sale avoids is the months of visible activity beforehand.
  2. Your lender has to be told if there is a mortgage, because it is redeemed from the proceeds on completion. That is handled solicitor to solicitor.
  3. Identity and source-of-funds checks are required under anti-money-laundering rules. Those checks are private to the firms involved, but they cannot be skipped, and you should be wary of anyone offering to skip them.

How a discreet sale works with us

  1. You get in touch. Fill in the short form or ring us. Nothing is recorded publicly and we do not pass your details to anyone.
  2. We ring you back. You will speak to someone who can make you an offer there and then, not a call handler.
  3. Written offer the same day. A real figure with the basis it rests on, valid for 14 days, with no obligation.
  4. One visit, at your convenience. Usually one person, usually under an hour. We do not turn up in branded cars.
  5. Solicitors handle the rest. You choose the completion date, typically 7 to 28 days.

When a discreet sale is not your best move

If your house is in good order, you are not under time pressure, and privacy is more a preference than a real concern, the open market will usually get you more money. We buy at a discount to market value in exchange for speed, certainty and no fees, and we are straight about that. A discreet sale is worth it when the privacy, the speed or the certainty genuinely matters more than the last few percent. If it does not, we will say so.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house without it being listed online?

Yes. We buy directly with our own funds, so the property never goes on Rightmove, Zoopla or any other portal. There is no listing to find and nothing for anyone to search.

Will there be a For Sale board outside my house?

No. We never put a board up. There is nothing outside the property at any point, before or after the sale.

Do I have to let strangers view the house?

No. There are no open-market viewings and no viewing appointments with members of the public. One of our team will usually want to see the property once, at a time that suits you, and that is the only visit.

Will my neighbours find out what I sold for?

Eventually, yes, and no buyer can honestly promise otherwise. Sold prices are recorded by HM Land Registry and published on their Price Paid data, usually two to six months after completion. What a discreet sale avoids is the months of visible activity beforehand: the board, the listing, the viewings and the questions. It does not erase the public record afterwards.

Can I sell discreetly if I still have a mortgage?

Yes. Your lender has to be told, because the mortgage is redeemed out of the sale proceeds on completion. That is a normal part of any sale and is handled between the solicitors. It does not make the sale public.

Is a discreet sale confidential from my family or my partner?

Not always, and we will not pretend otherwise. If the property is jointly owned, every owner has to agree to the sale and sign. If you are the sole owner, the decision is yours. Where a divorce or separation is involved there may also be legal duties of disclosure, and you should take your own advice on that.

Do you need identification and paperwork?

Yes. Anti-money-laundering rules require the solicitors to verify your identity and the source of funds. That is a legal requirement on any property sale in the UK. Those checks are private and go no further than the firms involved.

Is a discreet sale always the right choice?

No. If your property is in good order, you are not under time pressure and privacy is not a real concern, the open market will usually get you more money. We are worth talking to when speed, certainty or privacy matters more than the last few percent. We will tell you honestly if we think the open market suits you better.

How quickly can a discreet sale complete?

Typically 7 to 28 days from you accepting the offer, on a date you choose. There is no chain and no buyer's mortgage to wait for.

Do you buy discreetly across all of South Yorkshire?

Yes. Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley, plus the surrounding parts of Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire and Lincolnshire within roughly 20 miles.
