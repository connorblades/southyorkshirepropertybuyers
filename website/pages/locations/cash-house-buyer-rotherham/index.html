Compare what lands, not what is quoted

Compare what lands in your account, not what gets quoted. Three routes quote three different kinds of number. An estate agent quotes an asking price. That comes before their commission, before any price cut needed to agree a sale, and before months more bills on a house you're still paying for. Modern method of auction quotes a hammer price, but the buyer also pays a non-refundable reservation fee on top, usually 4% to 5% of the price plus VAT. It's called a buyer fee, which hides what really happens. A buyer works to one budget, so the fee comes out of what they can bid. Your own selling costs then come off that lower price. Our offer is the figure that reaches your account on completion. We aren't claiming you always end up with more by selling to us. We're saying the three numbers aren't like for like.

Six checks every Rotherham seller should make before accepting a cash offer

Nobody regulates cash buying as a sector, so a Companies House search and a fifteen-minute verification routine sit between you and the small minority of operators who damage the trade's reputation. Run these on us. Run them on every Rotherham buyer you speak to.

1. Companies House. Search the company name at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk. What you are looking for: active filings, current directors named, registered address, no late accounts.

2. Proof of funds. Ask for a letter from the buyer's solicitor, on letterhead, dated within 14 days of the offer. It should confirm the money is already sitting in their client account, and give the solicitor's SRA number. A screenshot of a bank balance isn't the same thing. Screenshots can be faked, edited or borrowed. A solicitor's client-account letter can't. The buyer's solicitor has to check where the money came from before completion anyway, under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017. A real buyer will send this letter when you ask. One who refuses, stalls or sends only a screenshot has told you what you needed to know.

3. SRA solicitor cross-check. Ask the buyer to name their solicitor in writing before you commit to anything. Then look the firm and the named person up at solicitors.lawsociety.org.uk. You want the firm regulated and in good standing, and that individual authorised. The SRA Standards and Regulations 2019 cover any solicitor handling a cash purchase. Conveyancing is simply the legal work of moving a house from your name into someone else's. A buyer using an unregulated "conveyancer" instead of an SRA-regulated firm is a red flag.

4. Reviews, and honest expectations. Search the company name on Google with the word "review", then again with the word "scam". Check Trustpilot and Google reviews. If the buyer's website has a team page with photos, drop those photos into Google Images to check they are real people and not stock images. Property fraud reports run into the tens of thousands a year in the UK, so being suspicious is sensible, not rude. We deal with our own review position honestly further down this page.

5. Your right to your own solicitor. You always have it. A real buyer is glad of it and never pushes you toward their own firm. We can cover your legal fees if you use our panel solicitor. If you'd rather use your own, we agree that case by case, but you never pay us a fee. Don't sign anything until your own legal adviser has read it. Any buyer who insists on a tied solicitor is looking after themselves, not you.

6. Three independent voices before you sign. Ring Citizens Advice Rotherham & District on 01709 515 680, or the national line on 0808 278 7911. StepChange is 0800 138 1111. MoneyHelper is 0800 138 7777. None of them are paid by us. All three will tell you, free, whether the offer in front of you looks fair, and whether selling to a cash buyer is right for you at all. Worried a buyer is offering sale-and-rent-back without permission? Check the FCA register at register.fca.org.uk and ring Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040.

A real Rotherham buyer will never ask you for any of these. An upfront fee. Your signature on something binding before you've had a written offer. A promise to use only their solicitor. Cash on the doorstep. Or a sale-and-rent-back pitch when they aren't FCA-authorised. Any one of them means walk away. They are not things to haggle over.

The FCA sale-and-rent-back warning: regulated since 2010, criminal if unauthorised

Sale-and-rent-back ("SRB") is where you sell your house and stay on in it as the tenant. It's usually pitched at people behind on the mortgage, facing repossession, or recently made redundant. It has been fully regulated since 2010, first by the Financial Services Authority and now the FCA, under MCOB 6A and CONC in the FCA Handbook.

Carrying on regulated sale-and-rent-back without FCA authorisation is a criminal offence under section 23 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Any Rotherham buyer offering "we'll buy your house and you stay as a tenant" must be FCA-authorised. Check the firm in 30 seconds at the FCA Financial Services Register (register.fca.org.uk). If they aren't on the register, walk away and report to the FCA and Action Fraud (0300 123 2040).

We don't offer SRB at all, and most legitimate cash buyers don't either. The rules exist because the arrangement has built-in problems: short tenancies, low sale prices, and nowhere else to live if the tenancy ends. Watch out if you've been made redundant at Speciality Steel UK / Liberty Steel, if you're behind on the mortgage, or if you're a landlord in one of Rotherham's new Selective Licensing wards. Those are exactly the sellers the unauthorised SRB pitch goes after. The law is clear. No FCA authorisation, no lawful SRB.

If you really do need to stay in the house, you have four routes. Regulated SRB through an FCA-authorised provider, though there are very few. A payment holiday or a longer mortgage term from your existing lender under the FCA Mortgage Charter. Support for Mortgage Interest. Or sell at full market value to a landlord and agree a tenancy with them separately. Get free independent advice first, always.
