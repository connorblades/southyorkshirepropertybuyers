How is the Chesterfield property market right now?

Prices are steady and a little higher than a year ago. The average Chesterfield home was worth £188,400 in February 2026 (HM Land Registry). That is a bit more than Barnsley and a good deal less than Sheffield, which is what you would expect of a town that acts as a Sheffield satellite.

How long a sale takes depends on where in Chesterfield you are:

  • Quickest: S42 Wingerworth, Holymoorside and Ashover. Usually a couple of months.
  • Steady: S40 Brampton and Walton. Around three months.
  • Slower: S41 Whittington and S43 Brimington. Often four or five months.
  • Slowest: the ex-coalfield streets in S43 Staveley and Inkersall, S44 Bolsover and older parts of S45 Clay Cross. Half a year is common, and about one seller in five gives up and takes the house off the market.

The bigger picture is steady but not easy. The Bank of England has cut rates three times from their peak, but borrowing is still dear and buyers are cautious. The Renters' Rights Act 2025 came into force on 1 May 2026. It scrapped Section 21 and replaced it with a Ground 1A notice: four months' notice, plus a ban on re-letting for 12 months if you decide to keep the property after all. A lot of small landlords in S41, S43, S44 and S45 are deciding to get out.

Chesterfield has Sheffield postcodes but Derbyshire councils. That matters when you sell. Chesterfield Borough Council covers S40, S41 and parts of S42 and S43. North East Derbyshire DC covers S45 and parts of S42. Bolsover DC covers S44 and parts of S43. Your council tax, the empty-homes premium, planning rules and your local court (Chesterfield Justice Centre, S41 7TD) are all Derbyshire. National cash-buying sites miss this, and several map the postcodes wrongly.

What are Chesterfield's distinctive sale challenges?

Some Chesterfield houses are far harder to sell through an agent than the market figures suggest. Usually it's because a lender won't touch them, and buyers give up rather than fight it. There are four reasons we see again and again.

The North Derbyshire Coalfield and CON29M mining searches

Almost every house in Chesterfield sits over old coal workings, and that scares off buyers who need a mortgage. The whole borough is inside the Mining Remediation Authority's Coal Mining Reporting Area. That body used to be called the Coal Authority. Collieries at Markham, Bolsover, Glapwell, Arkwright, Renishaw Park, Ireland, Williamthorpe, Holmewood, Grassmoor, Clay Cross and the Avenue Coking Works have all closed, but your right to claim for subsidence damage under the Coal Mining Subsidence Act 1991 still stands.

Your buyer's solicitor will order a CON29M mining search. It costs £35.10 including VAT and takes 5 to 10 working days. In S43, S44 and S45 it often comes back flagged: a mine entry within 20m of the house, an old subsidence claim, or shallow workings underneath. Lenders then want a mining survey before they will lend, and buyers drop out. We aren't borrowing to buy, so a flag doesn't stop us. That is why a cash sale is far more useful in Staveley, Bolsover and Clay Cross than the town-wide figures suggest.

HS2 cancellation and the Staveley story

Cancelling HS2 didn't wipe out Chesterfield house prices, whatever a buyer may tell you. The eastern leg was scrapped on 4 October 2023, taking the planned Staveley depot and the parkway station upgrade with it. The gain from HS2 had never really been priced into local houses, so there wasn't much to lose. What carried on: the Staveley Town Deal, the Clay Cross Town Deal, the new housing at The Avenue, and the Markham Vale employment park on the old Markham Colliery site at M1 J29A. The Station Quarter plan is being reworked around the Midland Mainline electrification, not dropped. Some cash buyers use HS2 as a scare tactic to justify a low offer. Don't fall for it.

Empty-home council tax premium across three councils

Leave a house empty for a year and your council tax doubles. All three councils charge a 100% premium once a home has stood empty for 12 months, rising to 200% after 5 years and 300% after 10, under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023. Chesterfield Borough Council charges for S40, S41, S43 (most) and parts of S42; Bolsover DC for S44; North East Derbyshire DC for S45 and parts of S42. Second homes pay the same premium. If you are dealing with a probate house standing empty, that 12-month clock is worth watching. The bill runs the whole time the estate is being sorted out, on top of the utilities, insurance and upkeep.

Tenant-in-situ exit, the Renters' Rights Act 2025, and the no-selective-licensing advantage

You can sell with the tenant still living there, which avoids the notice period altogether. The Renters' Rights Act 2025 came into force on 1 May 2026 and scrapped Section 21 "no-fault" evictions. To get the property back so you can sell it empty, you now serve a Ground 1A notice. That means four months' notice, plus a ban on re-letting for 12 months if you change your mind and keep it. Start to finish, six to eight months. Selling with the tenant in place skips all of that, because the new owner takes the tenancy on as part of the purchase. Distinctively for Chesterfield: Chesterfield Borough Council hasn't introduced a selective licensing scheme. Sheffield and Barnsley both do, so landlords there pay to register each property. Chesterfield landlords don't. HMO licensing still applies here. If you are deciding which property to sell first, Chesterfield is the lighter one to hold on to.

How to verify any Chesterfield cash buyer, including us

The cash-buying industry has people in it whom you wouldn't want to deal with. The Property Ombudsman, BBC consumer investigations and Action Fraud all describe a recurring set of predatory practices: price-chipping after survey, completion-day gazundering, FCA-unauthorised sale-and-rent-back, fake proof-of-funds, pseudo-local landing pages. A short, sensible check filters these out. None of it takes more than fifteen minutes.

  1. Companies House. Search at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk.
  2. Proof of funds. A solicitor's letter on letterhead, dated within 14 days, confirming cleared funds in client account. Screenshots of bank balances aren't the same.
  3. FCA register (only if sale-and-rent-back is on the table). Any buyer offering to buy your home and let you stay on as a tenant must be FCA-authorised, check register.fca.org.uk before signing anything. We don't offer sale-and-rent-back.
  4. ICO data protection registration. Every UK company processing personal data must be registered with the Information Commissioner's Office.
  5. Independent reviews. Honest acknowledgement: SYPB's public review density is still building. The other checks here matter more.
  6. Your right to your own solicitor. Always available. A legitimate buyer supports this and never pressures you toward only their nominated solicitor. The Property Ombudsman has flagged the "panel solicitor" model as a structural consumer harm. Citizens Advice Chesterfield (Saltergate), the SRA's solicitors register, and any local Chesterfield conveyancer can give you a free second opinion. None has any reason to recommend us.

We buy with our own money, we answer the phone ourselves, and we can be at a Chesterfield property within the hour. We use local Chesterfield SRA-regulated firms.

No legitimate buyer will ever ask you for an upfront fee or a deposit. None will ask you to sign anything binding before you have a written offer. None will insist you use their solicitor.
