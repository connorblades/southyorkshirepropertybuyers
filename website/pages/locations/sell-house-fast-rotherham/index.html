How is the Rotherham property market right now (2026)?

Prices in Rotherham are rising faster than in Sheffield, Barnsley or Doncaster. The average home here is worth around £197,400 (HM Land Registry UK House Price Index). Detached houses sit well above that and terraces well below. The borough average tells you very little about your own street.

How long a house takes to sell splits the borough into three:

  • Faster postcodes (S66 Wickersley/Bramley/Hellaby, S61 Thorpe Hesley/Greasbrough, S60 Moorgate/Broom): about 41 to 75 days to find a buyer, and most homes do sell.
  • Mid-market (S65 Dalton/Thrybergh/Ravenfield): about 56 to 70 days.
  • Slower postcodes (S63 Bolton-upon-Dearne, S64 Mexborough/Swinton/Kilnhurst, S66 Maltby, S62 Wath/Brampton/West Melton, S60 town centre/Eastwood): about 95 to 140 days, and around one in five listings comes off the market unsold.

Two other things matter. The Bank of England has cut rates back from their peak, but most people coming off a fixed deal this year still face a bigger monthly payment. That bites hardest in S60, S62 and S65, where steel jobs are common. The Renters' Rights Act 2025 also started on 1 May 2026.

What this means for you: Rotherham looks healthy on the headline figures. Underneath, it has the widest gap between its own postcodes of the four South Yorkshire boroughs. In S66 Wickersley or S61 Thorpe Hesley an estate agent usually leaves you with more money once fees are paid. In S63 Bolton-upon-Dearne, S64 Mexborough and Swinton, S66 Maltby, or any house with PRC, mining or condition problems, a cash sale often wins once you count the months of bills and the risk of the sale falling through.

Selling in Rotherham by postcode

The market in S66 Wickersley isn't the market in S63 Bolton-upon-Dearne. National competitor pages treat Rotherham as one place. It isn't. Here's how things actually differ across S60 to S66, with the S25 cross-border belt also covered.

Inter-war and post-war semis, plus Victorian terraces along Greasbrough Road. Thorpe Hesley and Scholes are newer estate detached homes, popular with Sheffield commuters using the M1 J35. About £105k to £280k. Kimberworth Park still has a lot of PRC concrete housing (Wimpey No-Fines, Tarran).

A postcode of two halves. Brampton and West Melton are former-colliery terraces and ex-council homes. Wentworth, Hoober and the rural fringe contain stone cottages and period detached around Wentworth Woodhouse. £105k-£420k. Coal Authority high-risk across most of the postcode.

Rotherham's cheapest postcode, roughly £68k to £155k. Old terraced pit housing built for Manvers Main, Wath Main, Hickleton Main and Barnburgh Main collieries. Pockets of PRC (Tarran, Airey, Reema) in Bolton and Adwick. Coal Authority high-risk comprehensive across S63.

S64: Mexborough, Swinton, Kilnhurst (cross-border Doncaster)

£75k-£170k. Mexborough proper sits in the City of Doncaster; the Kilnhurst end is Rotherham; Swinton is fully Rotherham. Sheffield-Doncaster railway line gives genuine commuter optionality. S65: East Herringthorpe, Dalton, Thrybergh, Ravenfield

£110k-£260k. Stable family-housing market with inter-war and post-war semi estates plus Dalton's Victorian core. Strong primary catchment in Dalton, Thrybergh and Ravenfield commuter villages. East Herringthorpe has higher deprivation indicators and a higher socially-rented share.

S66: Wickersley, Bramley, Maltby, Hellaby, Hooton Levitt, Aughton

£120k to £420k and up, and the split is sharp. Wickersley is the easiest part of Rotherham to sell in. Houses there go quickly, helped by the Wickersley School and Sports College catchment. Maltby is the lower end: ex-pit terraces and ex-council homes from the Maltby Colliery era (closed August 2013). Sales there take three months or more, and around one in five listings never sells.

S25: Aston, Aughton, Dinnington, Todwick, Anston

Cross-border belt straddling the Rotherham/Sheffield/Bassetlaw boundary. Modern estate development plus older village cores. £140k-£320k. Dinnington was added to the Selective Licensing scheme in February 2026.

What are Rotherham's distinctive sale challenges?

Some Rotherham houses are far harder to sell on the open market than the headline figures suggest. Mortgage lenders won't lend on them without conditions, and most buyers walk away rather than deal with those conditions. We buy a lot of these houses, for six reasons.

Coal Authority high-risk across S62, S63, S64 and S66

Almost the whole eastern half of the borough sits inside a Coal Mining Reporting Area. The Coal Authority was renamed the Mining Remediation Authority on 1 July 2024. Cortonwood, Manvers Main, Wath Main, Kilnhurst, Silverwood, Roundwood, Thrybergh Hall and Maltby Main were all in or next to Rotherham. The CON29M Mining Report costs about £35 including VAT and takes 5 to 10 working days. It often flags shallow workings less than 30 metres across S62, S63, S64 and S66. When it does, a lender will usually want a mining engineer's report or an insurance policy, or will simply lend less. We don't need a mortgage, so a mining flag doesn't stop us buying.

PRC and defective pre-cast concrete housing

The Housing Defects Act 1984, consolidated into Part XVI of the Housing Act 1985, formally designates certain pre-cast reinforced concrete (PRC) house types as defective. Rotherham types include Cornish Unit, Tarran, Airey, Reema Hollow Panel, Wimpey No-Fines, Wates, Unity, Boot, Smith and Whitson-Fairhurst, concentrated in pockets of S61 Kimberworth Park, S63 Bolton-upon-Dearne and S66 Maltby. Without a PRC repair certificate (the Code 6 / PRC Homes Limited certificate), mainstream mortgage lenders won't lend. Open-market sales of unmodified PRC stock routinely collapse at the lender's surveyor's report. We buy these without the certificate.

Selective Licensing, seven designated wards from 15 February 2026

Rotherham MBC's Selective Licensing Scheme started on 15 February 2026 and runs to 14 February 2031. It covers seven designated areas: Brinsworth, Dinnington, Rotherham town centre and Eastwood, Masbrough and Kimberworth, Parkgate, and Thurcroft. A licence costs £995 per property for the five years, with discounts for portfolios and for landlords rebated under the old scheme. Letting an unlicensed property in one of those areas is an offence under Housing Act 2004 Part 3. The penalty is up to £30,000, or an unlimited fine if you're prosecuted. Brinsworth, hit hardest by Liberty Steel, is on the list. If you sell with the tenant in place, the licensing question passes to the new owner.

Empty-homes 100% premium, activated 1 April 2026

From 1 April 2026 Rotherham charges double council tax on homes empty for 12 months or more, and on second homes. On an inherited Band D house that's roughly £2,300 to £2,500 a year on top of everything else. Rotherham is the last of the four South Yorkshire boroughs to bring this in, under the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023. Executors who were happy to hold a house for now often aren't once that lands.

Renters' Rights Act 2025. Ground 1A and the 12-month re-let ban

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 started on 1 May 2026 and scrapped Section 21 "no-fault" evictions. To get the property back so you can sell it empty, you now use Ground 1A. That means four months' notice, plus a 12-month ban on re-letting if the sale doesn't go through. For a Rotherham landlord that can mean a year of bills with no rent coming in. Plenty of small landlords are selling up because of it, mostly low-yield rentals in S63 and S64. Selling with the tenant in place avoids the Ground 1A notice and the re-let ban altogether. The new owner takes on the tenancy at completion under Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 s.3.

Japanese knotweed and TA6 disclosure after Patarkatsishvili v Woodward-Fisher

Japanese knotweed has spread along the Don corridor (Templeborough, Brinsworth, Aldwarke, Kilnhurst, Mexborough) and the Dearne corridor (Wath through Mexborough) since the 1980s. Houses within about 200m of either river are routinely flagged by surveyors. Patarkatsishvili v Woodward-Fisher [2025] EWHC 265 (Ch) means a buyer can sue if you hide a problem on the TA6 Property Information Form. That covers damp, infestation, knotweed, mining and past insurance claims. We buy anyway. You still have to tell the truth about what you know, but our offer doesn't depend on a clean knotweed report.

How to verify any Rotherham cash buyer, including us

The cash-buying industry has people in it whom you wouldn't want to deal with. A short, sensible check filters out the bad actors. None of it takes more than fifteen minutes.

  2. Proof of funds. A solicitor's letter on letterhead, dated within 14 days of the offer, confirming cleared funds in client account and quoting the solicitor's SRA number. Screenshots of bank balances aren't the same.
  3. Solicitor cross-check. Cross-check our (or any buyer's) solicitor at solicitors.lawsociety.org.uk: firm regulated, in good standing, named individual authorised.
  4. Independent reviews, honest acknowledgement. We don't yet have the public review density the largest national cash-buyer brands have built over a decade. The strongest verification we offer is the permanent Companies House record, our willingness to be checked at every step, and your right to use your own solicitor and your own independent advice before you commit.
  5. Your right to your own solicitor. Always available. A legitimate buyer supports this and never pressures you toward only their nominated solicitor.
  6. Three independent voices. Citizens Advice Rotherham & District (01709 515 680 / 0808 278 7911), StepChange (0800 138 1111) and MoneyHelper (0800 138 7777). None have any commercial reason to recommend us. For unauthorised sale-and-rent-back concerns: FCA Financial Services Register at register.fca.org.uk and Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040.
