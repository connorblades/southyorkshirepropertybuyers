Six checks every Sheffield seller should make before accepting a cash offer

Anyone can set up a cash-buying company and start advertising in Sheffield. There is no licence and no regulator for this industry, so the checking falls to you. Sheffield City Council Trading Standards has warned that rogue traders moved online behind "professional-looking websites which appear genuine, but many aren't". The six checks below take about twenty minutes and weed out the bad ones. Run them on us. Run them on every Sheffield cash buyer you speak to.

1. Companies House

Search the buyer's exact legal name, not the trading name, at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk. Check when the company was set up, because a brand new one is a warning sign. Check whether they file their accounts on time. You should also see a registered office, named directors, and anyone listed as a Person with Significant Control.

2. Dated solicitor's letter as proof of funds

Ask for a letter from the buyer's solicitor, on the solicitor's letterhead, dated within the last 14 days. It should confirm the money is there and ready. Don't accept a screenshot of a bank balance, because screenshots are easy to fake. A bank statement is weaker too, as the balance can change the next day. Then ring the solicitor to check the letter is real. Get the number from the Law Society Find a Solicitor register, not from the buyer. If a Sheffield cash buyer won't give you that letter, walk away.

3. Independent reviews, and a reverse image check

Search Google Reviews and Trustpilot for both the trading name and the legal name. Read the one and two star reviews first. They tell you more than the five stars. Then search the company name with the word "scam", and again with "complaint". If the website shows photos of the team, drag them into Google Images and see where else they appear. Stock photos or someone else's headshot mean you walk away.

4. The Property Ombudsman (TPO) membership

The Property Ombudsman is the official complaints scheme for companies that buy homes. Membership means the firm follows its code of practice and has a written complaints procedure. Check any claimed membership on the public register at tpos.co.uk. A TPO logo on a website with no entry on that register is a serious warning sign, and worth reporting to TPO.

5. Your absolute right to your own solicitor

You always have the right to use your own solicitor, whoever is buying. That is the most important line on this page. A real buyer is happy with it. A buyer who says you must use their solicitor is taking away your independent check, and that is a warning sign. We can cover your legal fees if you use our panel solicitor, and you can still say no and use your own. The solicitor who handles the paperwork on a house sale is called your conveyancer. The Sheffield firms below are a starting point, but any qualified conveyancer on the Law Society Find a Solicitor register will do.

6. One independent voice before you sign

Before signing anything binding, speak to one of the three independent voices a Sheffield seller can consult, all with no commercial interest in the outcome:

  • Citizens Advice Sheffield: freephone Adviceline 0808 278 7820 (Mon to Fri, 10am to 4pm), with face-to-face drop-in advice every Wednesday morning 10am to 12pm at the Community Access Point in Howden House, 1 Union Street, Sheffield S1 2SH.
  • The MoneySavingExpert House Buying, Renting & Selling forum, where real sellers describe what happened to them with quick-sale companies. Search the buyer's name. Search "we buy any house scam".
  • An unconnected Sheffield property solicitor, the three firms named in our solicitors section below, or any CQS-accredited firm on the Law Society register. A thirty-minute consultation costs far less than the difference between a bad deal and a good one.

A buyer who passes all six is one you can safely deal with. A buyer who fails any of them isn't, and you should walk away. That includes walking away from us, if we are the ones giving you the wrong answers.

The FCA sale-and-rent-back warning every Sheffield seller needs to read

Sale and rent back is regulated. Anyone offering it without FCA authorisation is committing a criminal offence.

Sale and rent back is where you sell your house and then stay on as a tenant. It has been regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority since 30 June 2010. Any firm offering it in Sheffield must be FCA authorised. Doing it without authorisation is a criminal offence under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. So if anyone offers to buy your Sheffield house and let you stay as a tenant, ask to see their FCA authorisation. If they can't show you, walk away. Report them to the FCA and through Report Fraud. We don't offer sale and rent back.

The rule exists for a reason. These deals were used to buy homes cheaply from people already in trouble, on short tenancies that made it easy to evict them a few months later. If a Sheffield buyer raises it and can't show you FCA authorisation, end the conversation.
