Compare what lands, not what is quoted

Compare what actually reaches your bank account, not the headline number. Three routes quote three different things, and none of them are like for like.

An estate agent quotes an asking price. What you keep is that price less their fee, less months of waiting, and only if your buyer stays the course.

A modern method of auction quotes a hammer price. On top of that the buyer pays a non-refundable reservation fee, commonly 4.2 to 5% of the price plus VAT. It's sold to you as "paid by the buyer, not the seller". That framing hides the point. A buyer has one total budget, so the fee comes out of what they can afford to bid. Your own costs then come off that lower price.

Our offer is the figure that lands in your account on completion. We aren't claiming you always end up with more with us. On a mortgage-free house that sells quickly, you probably won't. We're saying that until you compare what lands, you aren't comparing anything.

Six checks every Barnsley seller should make before accepting a cash offer

Run these checks on us. Run them on every cash buyer you approach. They are free, they take less than an afternoon, and they will save you from the small minority of operators who hope you won't bother.

1. Companies House, the permanent public record. Search the buyer's company name on find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk. Confirm the company exists, is active (not dissolved or in liquidation), has filed accounts, has identifiable directors, and wasn't incorporated in the last few months.

2. Proof of funds on a solicitor's letterhead. Ask for a letter from the buyer's solicitor, dated within 14 days of the offer. It should say the money for the purchase is already sitting in the firm's client account, and it should show the solicitor's SRA number. A screenshot of a bank or trading account is not proof. Solicitors have to check where a buyer's money comes from, under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017, so a genuine cash buyer's solicitor already holds this. Any buyer who can't or won't send it has failed the check.

3. SRA-regulated solicitor verification. Ask the buyer to name their solicitor in writing. Cross-check the firm and the named solicitor at the Law Society's Find a Solicitor directory. The firm should be in good standing with the Solicitors Regulation Authority. If the buyer's "solicitor" isn't on the SRA register, the transaction isn't safe.

4. Reviews and trading history. Read what other people say. Look at Trustpilot, Google, the Companies House filings and the Barnsley Chronicle archive. You want recent reviews that haven't been edited. Be wary of a brand-new Trustpilot profile, of piles of five-star reviews that all sound the same, and of any company whose whole online presence appeared in the last year.

5. Your own solicitor, confirmed in writing. A genuine buyer will put it in writing that you can use any solicitor you like. The SRA Code of Conduct (Standards and Regulations, paragraph 6.2) stops the same solicitor acting for buyer and seller, because their interests clash. If you use our panel solicitor, we can cover the legal fees. If you'd rather use your own, you can, and we think you should, whatever the house is worth. Any buyer who pushes you towards their solicitor, especially one they share an office or fees with, has failed this check.

6. Three independent voices. None of them work for us. Citizens Advice Barnsley, Doncaster and Rotherham covers the BDR area at citizensadvicebdr.org.uk, and the national Adviceline is 0808 278 7891. They won't recommend a buyer, but they will tell you what a proper sale should look like. Next, your own conveyancing solicitor. A first meeting usually costs £75 to £150 and is worth it. Conveyancing just means the legal work of moving the house from you to the buyer. And if you're selling after a death, ask for a STEP-qualified solicitor through the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners directory.

The FCA sale-and-rent-back warning

Never accept an offer that lets you stay on as a tenant unless the buyer is FCA authorised. This is called sale-and-rent-back, or SRB. It has been a regulated activity since 1 July 2010 under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Only firms authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority can offer it. A cash buyer offering it without that authorisation is committing a criminal offence.

The rules came in because people were badly hurt. An FSA investigation found sellers pushed into low prices, and tenancy agreements that weren't what sellers thought they had signed. It found rent rises and evictions soon after completion. Families ended up homeless, having already sold the only thing they owned. A small number of FCA-authorised firms still offer SRB properly, under supervision.

The check takes a minute. Before you accept any offer that involves staying on as a tenant, search the firm on the FCA Financial Services Register. If they aren't on it, the offer is illegal. Report it to the FCA on 0800 111 6768 and to Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040. We don't offer sale-and-rent-back. If staying on as a tenant is what you really need, go to an FCA-authorised SRB provider, not a cash buyer offering it on the side.
