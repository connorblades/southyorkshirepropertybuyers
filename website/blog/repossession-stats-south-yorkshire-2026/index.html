Repossession statistics in South Yorkshire 2026

Mortgage possession claims across Sheffield, Doncaster, Rotherham and Barnsley — what the court data shows, why South Yorkshire is disproportionately affected, and what options remain if you are at risk.

Written and reviewed by the South Yorkshire Property Buyers team. Published 7 September 2026.

Quick answer: Yorkshire and the Humber consistently records among the highest volumes of mortgage possession claims outside London. South Yorkshire’s four local authority areas — Sheffield, Doncaster, Rotherham and Barnsley — are all above the national average for possession claim rates relative to the housing stock. The Ministry of Justice publishes quarterly possession statistics with postcode-level data. If you are already in arrears and need to act fast, our stop repossession page explains how a cash sale can stop proceedings before the bailiff is instructed.

How mortgage repossession statistics are counted

The Ministry of Justice tracks mortgage possession activity in four stages, and understanding the difference between them matters:

  • Possession claims: the number of court applications your lender makes to begin legal proceedings. This is the earliest and largest figure.
  • Possession orders: the number of cases where the court grants the lender the legal right to take back the property. Many claims are resolved before an order is made.
  • Suspended possession orders: orders where the judge grants possession but suspends enforcement, giving the homeowner time to repay arrears under agreed terms.
  • Repossessions: the number of properties where the lender physically takes back the keys. This is the smallest figure — a fraction of the original claims.

When you hear that “repossessions are falling,” the headline figure usually refers to actual repossessions (the final stage). Claims filed — the starting point — can be rising even when repossessions appear stable, because lenders and courts are finding more resolutions along the way. Both figures matter.

The national picture in 2026

Mortgage arrears rose sharply through 2023 and 2024 as a wave of fixed-rate deals expired and borrowers remortgaged onto significantly higher rates. Between December 2021 and August 2023, the Bank of England Base Rate rose from 0.1% to 5.25% — a 5.15 percentage point increase in 20 months, the fastest sustained rate rise in 40 years. For a homeowner with a £150,000 interest-only mortgage, that alone added roughly £640 per month to their payment.

Rate cuts began in August 2024. By mid-2026, Bank Rate had fallen to around 3.75%, easing some of the pressure on variable-rate borrowers and those remortgaging. UK Finance, which tracks mortgage arrears for the industry, reported that arrears in the 2.5% or more of balance category peaked in late 2024 and began a gradual decline through 2025 and into 2026.

But the aggregate figures mask what is happening at a local level. Arrears and possessions are not evenly distributed. They concentrate in areas with lower household incomes, higher loan-to-value ratios, and a greater proportion of borrowers who stretched to buy. South Yorkshire is one of those areas.

South Yorkshire: why possession rates run above the national average

Several structural factors push South Yorkshire above the national average on possession claim rates:

Lower average incomes relative to house prices

South Yorkshire’s house price-to-income ratio is below the national average, but that masks a significant proportion of buyers who borrowed close to their affordability limit during the low-rate period of 2020 to 2022. When rates rose, those borrowers had less financial buffer than households in higher-income areas where price-to-income ratios look stretched but actual incomes are substantially higher.

Post-industrial economic base

Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley all have economies that were substantially rebuilt after the colliery closures of the 1980s and the steel contraction of the 1990s. While the region has diversified significantly, median wages remain below the English average in all four areas. A larger-than-average share of homeowners work in sectors with variable or seasonal income — logistics, hospitality, construction, retail — where a period of reduced earnings can rapidly tip arrears into default.

Higher proportion of older housing stock

South Yorkshire has a significant proportion of pre-1919 and inter-war housing. Older properties tend to have higher maintenance costs, and unexpected repair bills — a boiler failure, subsidence, roof repairs — can destabilise household finances that were already stretched by rising mortgage payments. This is a secondary cause of arrears that rarely appears in the headline statistics.

Buy-to-let landlord exits adding to court volumes

Since the Renters’ Rights Act 2026 came into force on 1 May, the volume of landlord possession claims has risen sharply across England. In South Yorkshire, where buy-to-let investment has been relatively active since the late 2010s, a significant number of small landlords have found that the combination of higher mortgage rates, reduced mortgage interest tax relief, and the new regulatory framework makes their portfolios unviable. Some are selling with tenants in situ; others are applying for possession to sell with vacant possession. This adds to overall court volumes, though it is a separate category from owner-occupier mortgage possession.

Sheffield

Sheffield is South Yorkshire’s largest city by population and housing stock. Mortgage possession claim rates within Sheffield city are not uniform: the S1 to S5 postcodes (city centre, Burngreave, Firth Park, Hillsborough) record higher rates than the S7 to S11 postcodes (Nether Edge, Ecclesall, Millhouses, Beauchief), which reflects the income differential across the city. Properties in the S61 to S66 range (Rotherham border postcodes including Meadowhall and Tinsley corridors) also appear in Sheffield County Court claim data.

Sheffield County Court is the principal venue for mortgage possession claims across the Sheffield and Rotherham local authority areas. Hearings are typically listed eight to twelve weeks after the claim is issued, though listing times vary with court capacity.

Doncaster

Doncaster recorded above-average mortgage possession rates through 2023 and 2024. The borough has a high concentration of former mining communities — Mexborough, Conisbrough, Bentley, Edlington — where the transition to the post-industrial economy has been slower than in Sheffield. Average household incomes in DN1 to DN8 postcodes are among the lowest in South Yorkshire, and loan-to-value ratios on mortgages taken out in 2020 to 2022 tend to be higher.

Doncaster County Court handles mortgage possession claims for the DN postcode area. The A1(M)/M18 corridor logistics economy has provided employment growth, but the work available to many ex-mining community households tends to be lower-wage and shift-based, with less income security than the salaried positions that logistics hubs generate at a national level.

Rotherham

Rotherham sits between Sheffield and Doncaster geographically and economically. The S60 to S66 postcode area covers a mix of urban Rotherham (S60, S65) and suburban and semi-rural areas (S62, S63, S64, S66). Possession claim rates are elevated in the urban core but more moderate in the outer areas, where incomes tend to be higher and housing stock newer.

The advanced manufacturing sector in Rotherham — centred on the Advanced Manufacturing Park at Waverley — has provided well-paid employment for a section of the local workforce. But the residents of Rotherham’s inner wards are largely not employed in that sector, and the income polarisation within the borough is significant.

Barnsley

Barnsley records the highest possession claim rate relative to housing stock of the four South Yorkshire local authorities in most recent MoJ reporting periods. The S70 to S75 postcode area covers former colliery communities where economic recovery has been slowest, and household incomes are the lowest of the four areas. Home ownership rates in Barnsley are relatively high — partly a legacy of Right to Buy — which means many households own their home but with limited financial reserves behind the ownership.

Barnsley County Court handles mortgage possession claims for the S70 to S75 area. The court has historically been busy relative to its size, and listing times for contested hearings can stretch considerably beyond the typical eight-week window seen in Sheffield.

What the statistics miss: the ‘hidden’ repossession pipeline

Court possession statistics only capture what reaches the formal legal process. A significant number of homeowners at serious risk of repossession resolve their situation before a claim is issued — through a voluntary sale, a remortgage, a payment plan agreed with the lender, or by engaging a cash buyer to complete before proceedings begin.

This means the true scale of mortgage distress in South Yorkshire is higher than the court figures suggest. We see it in our own enquiry volumes: the majority of homeowners who contact South Yorkshire Property Buyers about a repossession-risk sale have not yet received a court claim. They have received letters from their lender, a default notice, or a call from a debt recovery team, and they are looking for a way out before it goes further.

That matters for two reasons. First, it means more options are available. A homeowner who engages early — before a claim is issued, before a hearing date is set — has more time, more leverage with the lender, and more choice about how the situation resolves. Second, it means the national statistics undercount the number of households in genuine difficulty.

What to do if you are in arrears in South Yorkshire

Contact your lender immediately

Lenders who signed the Mortgage Charter agreed to offer specific options to borrowers in difficulty, including switching to interest-only payments temporarily, extending the mortgage term to reduce monthly payments, and a 12-month breathing space before beginning possession proceedings. Contact your lender as early as possible. The more notice you give, the more options remain open.

Get free debt advice

StepChange (0800 138 1111) and Citizens Advice are both free, independent, and confidential. A debt adviser can help you understand whether a payment plan is realistic, what your rights are at each stage of the possession process, and whether a formal debt solution such as a Debt Management Plan or Debt Relief Order would suit your situation. Do not delay getting advice because you are embarrassed or worried about the outcome — the outcome is always worse when you wait.

Consider a voluntary sale before proceedings begin

If the mortgage arrears are such that catching up is not realistic, selling the property voluntarily — rather than waiting for the lender to repossess — has significant advantages. You control the process. You choose the buyer. You keep any equity above what is owed on the mortgage and any associated debts. A repossession sale typically achieves well below market value, and the shortfall (if the property sells for less than the outstanding mortgage) remains your liability as a personal debt.

A cash buyer can complete in as little as seven days, which may be fast enough to stop proceedings even after a possession order has been made, provided the lender has not yet instructed bailiffs. If you are at this stage, contact a solicitor immediately: the window can close quickly and needs to be managed carefully.

Do not ignore court paperwork

If you receive a possession claim (a ‘N5’ claim form from the court), you have the right to defend the claim, negotiate with the lender, or make proposals to the court. Missing the deadline to respond, or failing to attend the hearing, almost always results in a possession order being made without any of your circumstances being considered. If you cannot afford a solicitor, contact your local Citizens Advice office immediately on receipt of any court paperwork.

Many possession claims are resolved — through repayment of arrears, a mortgage payment plan, or a voluntary sale — before the lender ever takes possession. A claim being issued does not mean repossession is inevitable.
