Repossession stats South Yorkshire 2026: what the data shows

Mortgage possession claims in England and Wales have been rising since 2022. Yorkshire and Humber consistently accounts for a disproportionate share. This guide explains what the Ministry of Justice data shows at a regional level, why South Yorkshire sits where it does, what the court pattern looks like in Sheffield and Doncaster, and what your options are if you are among the homeowners receiving a possession claim this year.

Written and reviewed by the South Yorkshire Property Buyers team. Published 10 August 2026 · Last reviewed: 10 August 2026. Data sources: Ministry of Justice Civil Justice Statistics Quarterly; MoJ Mortgage and Landlord Possession Statistics; ONS Regional Economic Indicators.

Quick answer: The Ministry of Justice's Mortgage and Landlord Possession Statistics show that mortgage possession claims in England and Wales rose each year between 2022 and 2025 as the effect of higher interest rates worked through fixed-rate deals. Yorkshire and Humber accounts for around 8 to 10 per cent of all claims, above its population share. Most claims, more than four in five nationally, resolve before a County Court Bailiff enforces an eviction. A voluntary cash sale is the single most reliable route to resolving a live possession claim while protecting whatever equity remains in the property. See our companion deep guide: how to stop house repossession in South Yorkshire.

What the national MoJ data shows

The Ministry of Justice publishes Civil Justice Statistics Quarterly together with the dedicated Mortgage and Landlord Possession Statistics series. These are the primary public source for understanding how many mortgage possession claims are being issued, how many result in an order, and how many end in an enforced repossession.

The trajectory since 2022 is clear. During the pandemic years 2020 and 2021, possession proceedings were heavily restricted by court moratoriums and lender forbearance obligations. When those restrictions lifted in 2022, claim volumes began rising again. They then accelerated as fixed-rate mortgage deals taken out at sub-2 per cent rates between 2020 and 2022 began expiring, with borrowers rolling onto rates of 4.5 to 6 per cent. The MoJ Q4 2025 data, the most recently published quarterly statistics at the time of writing, reported a national median of 46.7 weeks from claim issue to enforced repossession — broadly unchanged from the prior quarter and consistent with the court capacity picture across England and Wales. For the full breakdown of that 46.7-week timeline by stage, see our companion guide: Sheffield possession claims: the real court timeline.

Three figures matter most when reading the national statistics:

  • Claims issued: The number of times a lender or landlord has formally started court proceedings. Each claim represents a household in serious difficulty.
  • Orders made: The subset of claims where a District Judge has granted a possession order (either outright or suspended on terms). An order is not the same as an eviction — most suspended orders are never enforced.
  • Repossessions: The cases where a County Court Bailiff has actually attended and carried out an eviction. This is the final stage, and nationally it represents a small fraction of claims issued.

Yorkshire and Humber in the regional breakdown

The MoJ data is published at regional level for England (nine regions) plus Wales. Yorkshire and Humber is one of the nine English regions, covering approximately 5.5 million people across West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, North Yorkshire, and the East Riding.

Historically, Yorkshire and Humber accounts for around 8 to 10 per cent of all mortgage possession claims in England and Wales, modestly above the region's population share of approximately 9.6 per cent of the England and Wales total. The pattern is not dramatically above the national average in the way that, say, London or the South East can skew figures, but it is consistently elevated relative to the region's share of the overall mortgage stock.

Several structural factors explain this:

  • Earnings and affordability: Average full-time earnings in Yorkshire and Humber are below the England average. When mortgage rates rose from 2022 onwards, the squeeze on disposable income was proportionally sharper for households with a smaller earnings buffer above their fixed costs.
  • Housing stock age and condition: A significant proportion of owner-occupied properties in South Yorkshire were built between 1930 and 1970, with ongoing maintenance costs that can strain household finances. Properties in poor condition are also harder to refinance, which limits the options available to borrowers in arrears.
  • Employment base: While South Yorkshire has seen significant inward investment and regeneration since the decline of the steel and coal industries, employment in some postcodes remains concentrated in sectors with lower wages and less stability than the national average.
  • Interest-only and older mortgage products: Certain postcodes in Doncaster, Rotherham, and parts of Sheffield have a higher-than-average proportion of interest-only mortgages, some of which were arranged in the early 2000s when such products were common. These borrowers face a separate repayment problem as term end dates approach.

South Yorkshire: Sheffield, Doncaster, Rotherham, Barnsley

South Yorkshire comprises the four metropolitan boroughs of Sheffield, Doncaster, Rotherham, and Barnsley. Combined population is approximately 1.4 million, making it the largest urban area in Yorkshire and Humber and one of the largest in England outside London. Mortgage possession claims for these four boroughs are dealt with across two main court centres:

  • Sheffield Combined Court Centre, 50 West Bar, Sheffield S3 8PH: handles Sheffield, Rotherham, and Barnsley mortgage possession claims. Listings are in short blocks before a District Judge.
  • Doncaster Justice Centre, College Road, Doncaster DN1 3HS: handles Doncaster claims. Check listings via GOV.UK before attending as some business has been reallocated due to building condition issues identified from 2023 onwards.

What is driving arrears in South Yorkshire in 2026

The 2026 picture in South Yorkshire reflects several overlapping pressures that have been building since 2022:

Fixed-rate mortgage expiry wave

The UK mortgage market is dominated by fixed-rate products, typically two-year or five-year fixes. A large cohort of borrowers fixed at rates below 2 per cent between 2020 and 2022. As those deals expired, monthly payments on a typical South Yorkshire property could increase by £200 to £500 per month, depending on the outstanding balance and the new rate available. Borrowers who had manageable payments at 1.5 per cent found themselves stretched or unable to service the same mortgage at 4.5 to 5.5 per cent. While the Bank of England base rate has moderated from its 2023 peak, the lagged effect of those remortgage events is still working through the system in 2026.

Cost of living and payment priority

Elevated food, energy, and general living costs from 2022 onwards squeezed household budgets. For some borrowers, particularly those with children or caring responsibilities, day-to-day living costs were prioritised over mortgage payments, leading to arrears that accumulated gradually before triggering lender intervention.

Benefit changes and employment

Changes to Universal Credit and other benefit rates have affected mortgage interest support available to lower-income owner-occupiers. The Support for Mortgage Interest (SMI) loan scheme, which replaced direct mortgage interest payments in 2018, requires repayment on house sale and has low take-up rates in practice. Some borrowers in South Yorkshire in receipt of benefits have fallen through the gaps between the benefit system and the mortgage forbearance system.

Landlord possessions adding to the mix

The MoJ statistics cover both mortgage and landlord possession claims. Following the end of the section 21 no-fault eviction route in 2026 under the Renters' Rights Act, landlord possession claim volumes have been elevated as landlords sought to use section 21 before abolition, and now pursue section 8 grounds. This affects the housing market in a different way but adds to overall possession claim volumes at Sheffield Combined Court Centre and creates knock-on demand for emergency rehousing in the area. For landlords considering selling, see our guide: selling a tenanted property under the Renters' Rights Act 2026.

Most claims resolve before the bailiff arrives

One of the most important things to understand about possession statistics is what they do not show. The headline claim volume is not the same as the number of households who lost their homes. The Ministry of Justice data consistently shows that the gap between claims issued and enforced repossessions is large. Nationally, more than four in five mortgage possession claims issued at court are resolved without an enforced repossession by County Court Bailiff.

Resolutions happen in several ways:

  • Suspended possession orders: The most common outcome at a first hearing. The District Judge makes a possession order but suspends enforcement on terms, typically the normal monthly payment plus an agreed amount off the arrears each month. If the borrower keeps to the terms, the order is never enforced. The leading case is Cheltenham & Gloucester Building Society v Norgan [1996] 1 WLR 343, which established that the suspension period should normally be the remaining mortgage term.
  • Voluntary sale: The borrower sells the property before the bailiff appointment, repays the mortgage from the proceeds, and the claim becomes moot. This is the most common resolution where the arithmetic no longer supports staying: equity is protected, a county court judgment may be avoided, and the credit file damage is limited to the arrears period rather than including a possession judgment.
  • Refinance or capital raise: Some borrowers in arrears have sufficient equity to remortgage with a different lender or raise capital against the property to clear the arrears. This is harder to achieve in 2026 than in earlier years because of tighter affordability assessments and the fact that some lenders will not refinance a property subject to a live possession claim.
  • Arrears paid down: Some borrowers receive windfalls (redundancy payments, inheritance, benefit arrears owed to them) that allow them to clear or substantially reduce arrears and enter into a sustainable arrangement with the lender.

The existence of these resolution pathways is why acting early, before the hearing date, gives you options that disappear once the bailiff appointment is set.

How to read your possession claim documents

If a mortgage possession claim has been issued against your home, you will receive court documents by post. These will include:

  • Form N5 (Claim for possession of property): The claim form. It names the lender (claimant) and you (defendant), states the property address, and gives the hearing date and venue.
  • Particulars of Claim: The lender's statement of the debt and the grounds for seeking possession.
  • Form N11M (Mortgage: defence form): Your opportunity to respond before the hearing. You can set out your financial position, any repayment proposal, and any reasons you dispute the lender's figures.

The hearing date on the N5 form is the date that matters most. You have until that date to resolve the situation, file a defence, or attend with evidence of a solution. If you have a buyer in place, including a cash buyer who has issued a written offer with proof of funds, that is evidence the court can act on. A speculative mention of "I am thinking of selling" carries much less weight than a written offer letter and draft contract.

Your options once a claim is issued

Once you have received a possession claim, you have several routes open to you. The right one depends on your financial position, how much equity you have, whether you can sustain the mortgage long-term, and how much time remains before the hearing.

Option 1: Negotiate with your lender

Call your lender's arrears team and make a written proposal. Under MCOB 13 and the Pre-Action Protocol, lenders are required to consider reasonable proposals and must not pursue possession where a satisfactory arrangement can be made. A credible written proposal, particularly one supported by evidence of income, can result in the claim being stayed or withdrawn. If your lender refuses a reasonable proposal without good reason, that is relevant at court.

Option 2: Attend the hearing with a plan

Get free advice from HLPAS at the court door on the day of your hearing. District Judges at Sheffield Combined Court Centre are experienced in possession matters and will make a suspended possession order on realistic terms where the borrower can demonstrate a sustainable repayment plan. You do not need to be legally represented to achieve this outcome, though representation helps. See our detailed court guide for what to bring and what to expect: Sheffield court possession timeline 2026.

Option 3: Sell the property voluntarily

A voluntary sale runs in parallel to the court timetable. You do not need to wait for the hearing or get anyone's permission to sell. You do need to inform your lender in writing that you are selling, as this may cause them to agree a short adjournment of the proceedings while completion is arranged.

The arithmetic matters here. If the property has equity (the market value is higher than the outstanding mortgage plus arrears and costs), a voluntary sale will produce more money for you than an enforced repossession. Lenders typically recover the loan and costs from the proceeds, and any surplus belongs to you. An enforced repossession followed by a mortgagee in possession sale typically achieves less than a voluntary sale because the lender is selling for speed, not for best price.

Option 4: Cash sale on a short timetable

Where a hearing date is close or a bailiff warrant has been issued, a conventional open-market estate agency sale is unlikely to complete in time. Chain-dependent buyers, mortgage surveys, and conveyancing timelines typically add up to three to six months. A cash buyer removes the mortgage dependency and the chain. We can issue a written offer the same day and have contracts ready to exchange within days. Completion can follow within two to three weeks from exchange.

That is not a pitch to buy your house at any price. The offer will be below full open-market value because the service is speed and certainty rather than the highest possible price. The question to ask is not “is this below market value?” but “is this better than the alternative?” Where the alternative is an enforced repossession followed by a mortgagee in possession sale, residual arrears interest, possession and enforcement costs, and a county court judgment on your credit file, the arithmetic often runs in favour of a cash sale, even at a discount. Run the numbers with your solicitor before the hearing date.
