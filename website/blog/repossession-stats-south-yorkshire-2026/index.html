Repossession statistics in South Yorkshire 2026: what the numbers show

Mortgage possession orders across England and Wales rose 10% year-on-year in Q4 2025, the latest full quarter. Yorkshire and the Humber is tracking above the national rate. This guide explains what the court statistics actually show for Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley, why the rise is concentrated in specific postcodes, and what owners facing repossession can do to protect their position.

Published 28 September 2026 · Written and reviewed by the South Yorkshire Property Buyers team

Quick answer: Mortgage possession orders in England and Wales reached 5,614 in Q4 2025, up 10% year-on-year, according to the Ministry of Justice possession statistics. Yorkshire and the Humber is above the national trend. Within South Yorkshire, Doncaster and Barnsley have historically had the highest rates of possession claims per 1,000 mortgaged households. Sheffield's S2, S3 and S4 postcodes see the highest concentration within the city. If you have received a possession claim or a court date, the most important thing is to act quickly: lenders must offer forbearance options under the Mortgage Charter 2023 before a repossession proceeds, and a sale before the possession order is executed keeps equity in your hands.

Where does the repossession data come from?

The Ministry of Justice publishes quarterly mortgage and landlord possession statistics for England and Wales. They cover four stages of the possession process:

  • Claims — the lender applies to the county court for permission to repossess
  • Orders — the court grants permission, either outright or suspended on terms
  • Warrants — the lender applies to enforce the order (typically after the terms have been breached)
  • Repossessions — a bailiff physically executes the warrant and the owner loses the property

The published data is broken down by court area rather than local authority, which means Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley each align broadly with their respective county courts. The ONS also publishes arrears and possession data in its UK House Price Index and through the housing prices local dashboard. For lender-reported arrears, the FCA Mortgage Lending Statistics are the most granular source.

The Q4 2025 figures mark the steepest quarter-on-quarter acceleration since 2023. The backdrop is the maturity of two and five-year fixed deals taken out during the low-rate period of 2020-21. Borrowers who locked in at 1.5% to 2% and are now remortgaging at 4.5% to 5.5% are facing monthly payment increases of £300 to £600 or more. Those who couldn't refinance — due to affordability, credit profile or falling into negative equity — have been falling into arrears since late 2024.

Repossession statistics for Yorkshire and the Humber

Yorkshire and the Humber accounts for around 8% of England's mortgaged households but has historically produced 9 to 10% of national mortgage possession claims, reflecting above-average economic vulnerability in parts of the region.

Applying that ratio to the Q4 2025 national figures gives an indicative Yorkshire and the Humber figure of approximately:

  • Claims: around 830 to 920 in Q4 2025
  • Orders: around 500 to 560 in Q4 2025
  • Repossessions: around 115 to 130 in Q4 2025

These are estimates derived from national figures and regional share. The MOJ publishes court-level data in the underlying tables of their quarterly bulletin, which give the most precise local breakdown by court area.

South Yorkshire breakdown: Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley

Within Yorkshire and the Humber, the South Yorkshire Metropolitan County — covering the Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley local authority areas — accounts for the largest share of the region's mortgage possession activity.

Sheffield

Sheffield is the largest city and has the most mortgaged households. Its possession rate per 1,000 households is lower than Doncaster and Barnsley, reflecting a more economically diverse population and stronger employment in professional services, health and advanced manufacturing. However, within the city there are sharp local variations:

  • S2, S3, S4 (Manor, Park Hill, Burngreave, Grimesthorpe): highest arrears rates within Sheffield, reflecting lower average incomes and a higher concentration of ex-council properties purchased under Right to Buy with small deposits
  • S5, S6 (Southey, Hillsborough, Parson Cross): above-average arrears, similar structural factors
  • S10, S11, S17 (Broomhill, Nether Edge, Dore): very low arrears rates; these are higher-income owner-occupier areas where mortgage stress is less prevalent

The Sheffield court area has typically handled around 350 to 450 mortgage possession claims per quarter in recent years. The Q4 2025 acceleration suggests figures closer to the upper end of that range or above it.

Doncaster

Doncaster has the highest mortgage possession rate per household of the four South Yorkshire areas. The DN1 to DN8 postcodes (the town centre, Bentley, Mexborough, Thorne, Hatfield) see the highest activity. Three factors drive this:

  1. Lower average household incomes relative to Sheffield, limiting the buffer against payment shocks
  2. Higher reliance on Help to Buy purchases (2013-2023) at elevated LTV ratios, leaving less equity margin when values soften
  3. Flood risk in Bentley (DN5) and the Toll Bar/Fishlake corridor has made some properties harder to refinance, trapping owners on higher reversionary rates

The Doncaster court area typically handles around 200 to 280 mortgage possession claims per quarter. The 2025 trend suggests the figure is rising at a rate consistent with or above the national 10% increase.

Rotherham

Rotherham's position is broadly in line with the South Yorkshire average. The S60 to S66 postcodes cover a mixed economy town with a legacy of steel industry employment and a growing logistics and distribution sector. Possession rates in S63 (Wath, Bolton upon Dearne) and S64 (Swinton, Mexborough) are above the Rotherham average, reflecting pockets of lower-income owner-occupation. The S60 town centre area and S65 (Rawmarsh, Parkgate) also see higher rates than the more affluent S61 and S66 areas.

Barnsley

Barnsley has a possession rate per household that sits between Doncaster (the highest) and Sheffield (the lowest). The S70 to S75 postcode area covers the Barnsley town centre, Darton, Penistone and the surrounding ex-mining villages. Like Doncaster, Barnsley has a higher-than-average proportion of high-LTV purchases in the 2015-2022 period, leaving a cohort of owners with limited equity. Barnsley's proximity to the M1 and strong rental demand from commuters (Leeds, Sheffield) has also meant more buy-to-let landlord possession activity relative to its size.

Landlord possession statistics in South Yorkshire

The MOJ statistics also cover landlord possession claims — section 21 no-fault notices and section 8 rent arrears grounds. The Renters' Rights Act 2025, which commenced on 1 May 2026, abolished section 21. This has shifted landlord possession activity entirely onto section 8 grounds, primarily Ground 8 (mandatory, two months' rent arrears) and Ground 1A (landlord intending to sell or move in).

The implication for the repossession statistics is that a number of the possession claims appearing in 2026 MOJ data will be Ground 1A landlord sales rather than mortgage arrears. In Barnsley, Rotherham and Doncaster — where buy-to-let ownership is proportionally high — this adds a further category of forced or semi-forced sales to the market. For landlords in this position, the choice is between a drawn-out court possession process followed by a traditional sale and a direct cash sale with vacant possession now.

Why are repossessions rising faster in some South Yorkshire postcodes?

Five structural factors explain the concentration of possession activity in specific South Yorkshire postcodes.

1. Legacy of Right to Buy high-LTV purchases. Many properties in Barnsley, Doncaster, S2 and S5 were purchased under Right to Buy in the 1990s and 2000s at modest prices, then inherited or resold to buyers using small deposits. When those buyers came to remortgage in 2024-25 at higher rates, many found the monthly payment increase unmanageable on static wages.

2. Help to Buy concentration. The Help to Buy equity loan scheme ran from 2013 to 2023 and was heavily used in lower-price new-build areas. Doncaster and Barnsley new-build completions under the scheme were proportionally high. The equity loan element must be repaid at 20% of the property's then-current market value, meaning owners who needed to sell or remortgage in a flat or falling market faced negative equity exposure at the point of exit.

3. Flood-affected postcodes unable to remortgage. Properties in the Bentley (DN5), Toll Bar, Fishlake and Fishlake Flyer corridor have faced difficulty refinancing since the 2019 and 2020 flooding events. Some insurers withdrew cover; others imposed premiums or excess levels that lenders won't accept. Owners stuck on standard variable rates have seen payments rise faster than owners who could remortgage.

4. Ex-mining ground risk and specialist mortgage refusal. Around 80% of Barnsley and 75% of Rotherham sit within a Development High Risk Area on the Coal Authority's CON29M mining search. Some lenders won't lend on properties with unmitigated shallow workings, effectively trapping those owners on their existing lender's reversionary rate. If that lender raises rates or refuses to offer a new product, the owner has no remortgage option on the open market.

5. Wage stagnation in ex-industrial areas. Real wages in Sheffield S2-S5, Rotherham S60-S65, Barnsley S70-S74 and Doncaster DN1-DN8 have grown more slowly than in Sheffield's professional postcodes or South Yorkshire's logistics employment belt. The combination of static income and a doubling of mortgage costs is unsustainable for a cohort of households that had no savings buffer going into the rate cycle.

What to do if you are facing repossession in South Yorkshire

The steps below apply whether you are at the early arrears stage or have already received a possession hearing letter.

Step 1: Contact your lender immediately. Under the Mortgage Charter 2023, all major UK lenders are required to offer at least one of the following before proceeding with possession: a payment holiday of up to six months, a term extension to reduce the monthly payment, or a temporary switch to interest-only. You need to ask — these protections are not automatic. Your lender's arrears team number is on your mortgage statement or the lender's website.

Step 2: Get free debt advice. The three main free services are:

  • StepChange Debt Charity — 0800 138 1111 (free from landlines and mobiles)
  • Citizens Advice — local offices in Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and Barnsley
  • National Debtline — 0808 808 4000

These services are independent and can often negotiate directly with lenders, which moves faster than going through the court process yourself. Do not pay for debt advice — all three above are free.

Step 3: If you have received a possession claim, attend the hearing. A possession hearing is not a final order. You can attend, explain your position and ask for a suspended order (which gives you time to clear the arrears in instalments). If you do not attend, the court will almost always grant the order in your absence.

Step 4: Consider selling before the order is executed. If the arrears are unrecoverable and the lender has obtained or is about to obtain a possession order, selling the property yourself protects three things: it preserves any equity above the mortgage balance (a lender selling under power of sale prioritises clearing the debt, not maximising your return), it prevents the most damaging county court judgment from appearing on your credit file, and it gives you control over the timeline. A cash sale can complete in 7 to 28 days — fast enough to beat most repossession dates once the possession date is known.

What happens if the lender repossesses instead?

If the possession order is executed and the lender takes back the property, several things happen that most people don't realise until it's too late.

First, the lender sells under its power of sale. In Cuckmere Brick Co Ltd v Mutual Finance Ltd [1971] Ch 949, the Court of Appeal held that a lender selling under power of sale must take reasonable care to obtain the true market value. In practice, this often means an auction sale at a price lower than a private sale would achieve — not necessarily unlawful, but frequently less than the market.

Second, the lender takes what it needs to clear the mortgage balance and its costs. If the sale proceeds are insufficient, the shortfall is a personal debt that continues to be chased. This is a mortgage shortfall debt and it can be pursued for up to 12 years under the Limitation Act 1980.

Third, the possession order creates a county court judgment (CCJ) that stays on your credit file for six years. This makes it very difficult to rent a home privately, obtain credit, or remortgage another property within that period.

Selling before the possession order is executed avoids all three of these outcomes.
