Cash buyer vs auction: compare fees, timing and sale terms

An auction and a direct cash sale offer different ways forward. Compare the fees, commitments and work still needed, especially if a buyer has withdrawn, the house needs major repairs or you have a real deadline.

Published 4 May 2026. Updated 10 November 2026. South Yorkshire Property Buyers team.

Quick answer: Compare who is buying, the fees you pay, the point at which the sale becomes binding and the work needed before completion. Keep traditional auction, modern conditional auction and a direct cash sale separate. None is automatically the right route for every seller.

Start with the problem the sale needs to solve

If you have already spent time preparing the home or lost a previous buyer, another selling decision can feel like starting again. You may need a route that fits an onward move, an estate responsibility or repairs you cannot take on. Put that practical need alongside the written terms, rather than rely on a headline promise about speed or certainty.

This is a decision checklist. For the detailed process, read our guide to selling a house at auction. If you are looking specifically at a conditional auction, use the modern method of auction guide.

Keep the three routes separate

What a seller should compare
RouteCommitment to checkCosts and timing to check
Traditional unconditional auctionThe successful bid normally creates a sale contract under the auction conditions.Preparation, marketing, deposit and completion terms, seller charges and the legal pack.
Modern conditional auctionA reservation agreement comes before exchange of the property sale contract and can create obligations of its own.Who pays each fee, the timetable trigger, exclusivity, extensions and what happens if the buyer withdraws.
Direct cash purchaseAn ordinary accepted offer remains subject to contract until exchange.Evidence of funding, property and legal checks, written conditions and a realistic agreed timetable.

The government's guidance on offers and negotiations explains exchange in England and Wales. For a conditional auction example, iamsold's reservation terms include obligations before the property sale contract is exchanged. Your own agreement, special conditions and solicitor's advice matter.

Compare your charges separately from the buyer's fees

Ask the auctioneer for an itemised written breakdown of entry or marketing charges, commission, VAT, legal pack preparation and any withdrawal or unsold charges. Auction House's seller cost guidance makes clear that arrangements depend on the auction room and property.

A buyer may pay a reservation or other charge in addition to the purchase price. That affects the buyer's overall budget, but it is not automatically a seller fee or a matching reduction in the bid. Do not deduct it from your proceeds as though you were paying it. Check the actual fee structure and who receives each payment.

For our direct purchase, there is no charge to enquire and no obligation to accept an offer. We charge no estate agent fees and can cover legal fees if you use our panel solicitor. You can choose your own solicitor and pay their fees instead. Mortgage repayment, existing agreement charges and any applicable tax remain separate considerations.

Compare the full timetable

An auction's advertised transaction period may start after preparation and marketing. Ask what starts the clock, which work must happen before exchange and what could extend the deadline. For a direct purchase, ask the same practical questions about access, funding, title and the solicitor's remaining work. A buyer without a mortgage or onward sale removes those dependencies, but not every possible delay.

If there is probate to finish, a tenancy, a legal notice or a major defect, explain it early. A route cannot make those issues disappear. Tell each provider why your deadline matters and ask what still needs to happen before anyone can rely on a completion date.

Already unsold, or has a buyer pulled out?

A reserve not being met, a reserved buyer withdrawing and a problem after exchange are different situations. Obtain written confirmation of the current position. Ask your solicitor to check exclusivity, termination, introduced buyer clauses and outstanding charges before switching route.

If this is your situation, see your next steps after an unsuccessful modern auction. If an ordinary sale collapsed, read what to do after your buyer pulls out. We can discuss a possible direct purchase without assuming your previous agreement has ended.

Five things to gather before choosing

  • The auction or agency agreement and any reservation paperwork.
  • Known property issues, surveys and relevant legal documents.
  • A written list of charges, including what happens if the sale stops.
  • Evidence of the proposed buyer's funding and any chain.
  • Your real deadline and the work your solicitor says remains.

Need another option to compare?

Tell us about the home, what has happened so far and when you need to sell. We will explain whether a direct purchase could help and the conditions of any written offer.

Ask about a direct cash offer

Common questions

Neither route guarantees a better result. Compare the actual written terms, your own costs, the buyer's funding and the steps still needed. A guide or an accepted offer is not the same as a completed sale.

Ask for an itemised list of marketing or entry charges, commission, VAT, legal pack costs and any charges if you withdraw or the property does not sell. Keep fees payable by you separate from charges payable by the buyer. Your agreement and the property terms determine what applies.

Include preparation and marketing as well as the period after a buyer is found. An auction deadline depends on its conditions and starting event. For a direct sale, discuss the work still needed with the buyer and your solicitor. No route should be treated as a guaranteed completion date before the property and legal position have been assessed.

Ask the auctioneer and your solicitor to confirm whether the property is unsold, reserved, exchanged or still subject to an agreement. Check continuing exclusivity and charges before accepting another buyer. A reservation fee is not automatically money payable to the seller.

The reserve may not be met, a reserved transaction may stall, or legal and property issues may affect progress. A direct offer also has conditions and can be affected by checks before exchange. Ask what happens if a sale does not complete and what costs remain.

Consider the property, your actual deadline, the work you can take on and the agreements already in place. Auction may suit competitive marketing. A direct purchase may be worth comparing after a failed sale or for a home needing substantial work. Take advice on a probate or other restricted sale before committing.

A traditional unconditional auction normally creates a binding sale contract when the auction ends under its conditions. Modern auction usually begins with a reservation agreement before the sale contract is exchanged. The reservation can create binding obligations of its own. Read the individual terms with your solicitor.

Ask your auctioneer and solicitor whether the agreement allows it, which fees would apply, how the lot would be withdrawn and what contract the proposed buyer would sign. A conversation with another buyer does not end an existing instruction or reservation agreement.

An ordinary accepted offer in England and Wales is not binding until contracts are exchanged. Cash funding removes a mortgage dependency but does not remove property or legal checks. Separate reservation, exclusivity or agency agreements may create obligations or costs before exchange, so have your solicitor review the terms.
