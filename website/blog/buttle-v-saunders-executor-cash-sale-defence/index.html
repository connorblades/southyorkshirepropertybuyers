Buttle v Saunders: what executors should check before a sale

Choosing a buyer for an estate can feel more difficult when other people will review the decision. Understand the legal question, keep a clear record of the options and take advice before committing to a property sale.

Published 23 May 2026. Updated 10 November 2026.

Quick answer: Buttle v Saunders is a case about fiduciary decision making, not automatic protection for accepting a cash offer. An executor should have the estate solicitor review authority, competing proposals and the reasons for the decision. A file note or statutory notice does not by itself establish that a sale decision fulfils the estate duties.

What does Buttle v Saunders explain?

Buttle v Saunders is cited as [1950] 2 All ER 193. A later English High Court judgment, Forstater v Python (Monty) Pictures Ltd, paragraph 148, discusses the case. It describes trustees who felt bound by their earlier understanding with a buyer despite a late higher offer. That feeling could not itself justify their decision; the appropriate response depended on the facts.

The useful lesson for an estate sale is to take the decision in the proper capacity and consider relevant evidence. The original case involved trustees. Applying its principles to a particular executor, will, property and proposed contract needs legal advice. It does not supply a blanket approval for a particular kind of buyer.

Compare proposals with advice, rather than a buyer label

Ask the estate solicitor what evidence is needed to assess the offers and the estate's responsibilities. The amount offered, conditions, funding, property issues and legal readiness all need to be understood. Do not assume a higher headline proposal must be accepted without examining it, or that speed alone justifies choosing a different proposal.

A cash buyer can explain its funding and written conditions. It cannot certify that the executor has fulfilled their duties by accepting its offer. Obtain independent professional advice where needed, and keep any disagreement or conflict of interest visible to the estate solicitor.

Keep a record that another adviser can follow

A record is useful when it explains what was known and why the decision was made. It is not a legal guarantee. Discuss the scope of the record with the estate solicitor rather than treating a buyer's template as sufficient.

  • Who has authority to act, and which will or title requirements have been checked?
  • What professional advice and property information were available?
  • Which written proposals were received, including their conditions and funding?
  • Were there unresolved defects, tenancy issues, existing agreements or competing offers?
  • What practical deadline or estate cost was relevant, and what evidence supports it?
  • Who considered the decision, what concerns were raised and what advice was taken?

Keep the underlying documents as well as a summary. A later change in an offer, condition or legal issue may require the decision to be reviewed.

What section 27 notices do and do not establish

Section 27 of the Trustee Act 1925 addresses certain unknown claims when property is conveyed or distributed. Its protection is subject to the statutory conditions and does not settle every question about administering an estate.

A notice does not establish that a proposed sale price is adequate or remove the need to fulfil the estate's duties. Ask the estate solicitor whether notices are appropriate and what other enquiries or safeguards are required. Publishing a notice and writing a file note are not a safe harbour for a sale decision.

If people disagree or another offer arrives

Keep the correspondence and tell the estate solicitor before making a commitment or dismissing the new information. They can advise who must act, what the will and title require, whether there is a conflict and how concerns should be addressed.

Do not reduce the issue to a family vote or a blanket rule about a beneficiary's veto. The legal position depends on the estate and any existing commitments. If an agreement has already been signed, provide it to the solicitor before changing direction.

Common questions

The case is discussed in Forstater v Python at paragraph 148. Trustees could not justify completing an earlier arrangement solely because they felt honour bound to it after a late higher offer. The proper response depended on the facts. Take legal advice on the proposals and the estate responsibilities.

Do not use a simple rule detached from the actual proposals and duties. Have the estate solicitor review the evidence, terms, funding and relevant circumstances. A buyer cannot approve a different offer as legally safe simply because it pays cash or proposes a faster timetable.

Keep authority and title information, professional advice, property documents, written offers and conditions, funding evidence, relevant correspondence and the reasons considered. Agree the approach with the estate solicitor. A record supports understanding the decision but does not guarantee protection from a claim.

Section 27 concerns certain unknown claims when estate property is conveyed or distributed. It does not establish that a proposed sale price is adequate or that the executor has fulfilled every duty. Ask the estate solicitor what notices and other checks are appropriate.

Record the concern and seek advice before committing or changing buyer. The will, title, authority, conflicts and existing agreements may matter. Avoid assuming that either a family vote or one representative acting alone resolves every estate sale decision.

A cash offer does not automatically protect an executor's decision. It is one proposal whose funding, conditions and practical consequences can be assessed. The estate solicitor should advise on authority, duties and evidence before the estate commits.
