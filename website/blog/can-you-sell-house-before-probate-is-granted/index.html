Can you sell a house before probate is granted?

If a house needs to be sold but the estate paperwork is still in progress, it is natural to want a clear next step. Begin with who owns the property and who can act, then separate preparation from making a commitment.

Published 27 April 2026. Updated 10 November 2026.

Quick answer: First establish whether a grant is needed and who has authority to act. GOV.UK advises waiting until probate before marketing property or making financial plans. Executors, administrators and surviving joint owners do not all have the same position. Have the estate solicitor confirm what can happen before approaching a binding contract or relying on a completion date.

Start with ownership and authority

A property in an estate is not always dealt with in the same way as a property with a surviving joint owner. The will, the title and the people entitled to act matter. A family member expecting an inheritance should not assume that expectation gives them authority to sell.

HM Land Registry's practice guide on the death of an owner makes an important distinction for a sole registered owner: an executor's title arises on death, but probate is needed to prove entitlement before the Registry accepts their transfer or registers them. An administrator's position is different, and the Registry will not accept a disposition predating the letters of administration. These rules need to be applied by the conveyancer to the actual title and estate.

What can you prepare while the position is checked?

Useful preparation does not require a promise that a sale can complete on a particular date. Gather what the estate solicitor and any proposed buyer would need to understand the home.

  • Find the will, death certificate and application correspondence.
  • Identify the representatives and who is handling the estate and conveyancing.
  • Locate title documents, mortgage details and any tenancy or existing sale agreement.
  • Record known defects and find surveys, notices or repair paperwork.
  • Check the insurance, security and arrangements for access to an empty property.

Keep a simple list of confirmed facts and open questions. That helps avoid different relatives or advisers working from different assumptions.

Marketing, offers and exchange are different steps

GOV.UK's probate guidance advises waiting until probate before putting property on the market or making financial plans. Ask your solicitor how the ownership and estate position affect your next action. A generic website cannot clear a particular pre grant transaction.

For an ordinary sale in England and Wales, accepting an offer is different from exchanging binding contracts. Separate agreements can also create obligations. Do not sign a reservation, exclusivity or conditional sale arrangement on the assumption that the grant will arrive in time.

If someone proposes exchange before a grant is available, have the estate's own solicitor assess the authority, wording, deadlines and consequences before anything is signed. This is not a standard shortcut that a cash buyer can approve for you.

What if there is a surviving joint owner?

The legal title and the deceased person's beneficial interest need to be distinguished. GOV.UK explains joint tenants and tenants in common. The ownership arrangement affects what passes on death; it is not safe to assume every jointly owned home follows the sole owner probate route.

Give the conveyancer the title information and details of the death so they can check the survivor's position, any trust or restriction and the evidence needed for a sale. Avoid using a simple label such as joint ownership as permission to proceed without those checks.

If a buyer is already waiting

Tell the estate solicitor exactly what has been agreed, what has been signed and whether any money has changed hands. Keep the buyer and their conveyancer informed through the appropriate advisers, with a clear explanation of outstanding documents.

Ask for the current position before promising a date, extending a deadline or changing buyer. A delay after a contract or separate agreement has been signed can have consequences that depend on its terms. Do not assume the arrangement pauses automatically.
